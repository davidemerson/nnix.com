# overview
The burn box is a place to burn small papers safely indoors. It's a small open-topped steel box, four panels welded to a base plate, and the base is drilled with vent holes so the fire can pull fresh air in from underneath while it's going.

That's the whole idea. Something you can set a note on fire in, on a table, without setting anything else on fire.

The panels are stamped a word to a face: LIFE EXISTS AND IDENTITY. It's Whitman, from "O Me! O Life!", where the answer to the question is that you are here, that life exists and identity, that the powerful play goes on and you may contribute a verse.

# build
The panels are thin sheet steel, tacked at the corners first and then welded out, with the vent holes drilled through the base after the box was closed up.

Thin metal was the real lesson here. It's far less forgiving on flux core MIG than thick metal is. On heavy stock you have plenty of latitude in both time and power: you can dwell, you can run hot, and the mass around the puddle carries the heat off. None of that is true on thin material. Dwell a half second too long or run a touch too hot and you're not welding anymore, you're cutting a hole and then trying to fill it back in. Good exercise.
